"auth language" = /ˈɔːθ ˈlæŋ.ɡwɪdʒ/ (also known as "aut language", or "auty language") is basically Canadian English (basically British English) if a bunch of common words got turned into uwu-kawaii-so-cute words.

If you are American, British English is English, if you introduced the letter "u" to some random words and made things end with a -re or -se. W-wait, don't go! I swear I'm not your boring English teacher, come back! RIGHT NOW!

But seriously, do your own research on British English if you genuinely don't know what it is.

the actual words

The most common words are:

wawwoe
...a word to express happiness or excitement, or anything in-between that is positive. It can be used at any moment and anytime, and can be used however you want.
watafakies
...a word to express disgust, hate, or anything in-between that is negative. It can be used at any moment and anytime, but cannot be used in any way -- it has to be by itself.
woa
...a word to express fascination. Usually paired with a series of periods after (etc: woa.........). The more periods used, the more the person is fascinated by a work, art, or actions.
okeh (or oke)
...a replacement for the word "Ok" or "Okay".
stawp
...a replacement for the word "Stop".
berry
...a replacement for the word "Very". Depending on the context, it could mean the fruit too.
wat
...a replacement for the word "What".
liek
...a replacement for the word "Like".
da
...a replacement for the word "The".
naow
...a replacement for the word "No".

Usually when things get serious, you can just switch to normal English and stop using auth language.
